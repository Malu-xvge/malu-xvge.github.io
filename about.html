About

A small studio in Lilongwe.

ALUM builds Android apps for people who have one phone, a shared data bundle, and better things to spend it on than a subscription.

Who we are?

Proudly Malawian. Everything — the games, the questions, the artwork, the code — is designed and built here in Malawi. That is deliberate. Software written for this market by people who do not live in it tends to feel borrowed: translated jokes, expensive data plans, card payments nobody has.

How we build

  • Offline first. If a feature needs a connection to be useful, it is probably not the feature we should build. The content ships inside the app.
  • Small and cheap. The apps are a few megabytes and run on older Android phones. Nothing asks you to upgrade your handset.
  • One-off payments. Buying something is a single mobile money payment, not a subscription. Nothing renews behind your back.
  • No accounts. You do not sign up, so there is nothing to log into, nothing to forget, and no profile to protect.
  • Direct downloads. Apps are published here as APK files, so they are not held hostage by a store's rules or removed overnight.

Why direct downloads

Publishing on this site means you install the file straight from us, and an update never disappears because a review went the wrong way. It also means we can charge for a pass with mobile money — something the big app stores do not allow. The trade-off is that Android shows a warning when you install, and you have to allow installs from your browser once. Every download lists its size and a SHA-256 checksum so you can confirm the file is exactly what we published.

What we have released

GameNight — six party games for one phone: Trivia, Charades, Dares, Quick Challenge, Ndagi and Apa Ndipati. Released November 2026, still being updated.

What is next

More apps are in progress, including tools rather than games. They will appear on the downloads page when they are ready — we would rather ship one thing that works than five that nearly do.
